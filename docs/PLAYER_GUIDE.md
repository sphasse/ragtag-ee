# Player Guide: Welcome to the Drift

You and your crew are aboard an independent freighter in **the Drift**—a cluster of five suns at the edge of settled space where money is tight, law is far away, and everyone owes someone.

This guide explains the universe, your ship, and how to survive.

## Your Ship and Crew

You're aboard a **light freighter**—small, scrappy, and (if you're lucky) paid for. It has:
- **Impulse engines** for local travel (~0.1u per second, where 1u = 1000 distance units)
- **Warp drive** for faster travel between stations (if you have fuel)
- **Jump drive** (if you buy the upgrade) for instant long-range jumps
- **Shields** (front and rear) that regenerate but fail under sustained fire
- **Hull** that doesn't heal on its own (requires docking or repair crew)
- **Weapons**: beam weapons (short range, auto-fire) and missile tubes (longer range, limited ammo)
- **Power and coolant systems** that Engineering manages

### Your Stations (6 roles)

Each player controls one station:

| Station | What You Do |
|---------|-------------|
| **Helm** | Steer the ship, control impulse/warp/jump, dock at stations |
| **Weapons** | Aim beam weapons, load/fire missiles, manage weapon power |
| **Engineering** | Manage power distribution, route coolant, repair damaged systems |
| **Science** | Scan objects (reveals details), analyze threats, provide intel |
| **Relay** | Read sector map, set waypoints, monitor communications, track allies/enemies |
| **Operations** | (Optional) Help Science with scanning and Relay with comms |

**Captain** doesn't have a console—they walk around, coordinate, and make decisions.

## The Universe: The Drift

![The Drift Map](verse_map.svg)

Five suns span the Drift, from the rich **Concord Core** to the deadly **Maw**.

### The Five Suns

1. **Lumen** (white sun) — The Core. Rich Concord worlds. Safe, expensive, heavily policed.
2. **Cardinal & Cinder** (yellow/red suns) — The Border. Independent trading posts around Port Vesper. Busy, messy, neutral ground.
3. **Kestrel** (gold sun) — The Rim. Frontier worlds. Long distances, rough people, opportunities.
4. **Pale Sun** (blue sun) — The edge. The Maw is out here. Nobody comes back.

### Key Locations

**Safe Starting Areas:**
- **Port Vesper** (0, 0) — Independent trading hub. Busy, neutral, sells basic upgrades. Your likely starting point.
- **Halcyon Rest** — Waystation at the edge of the Shroud nebula. Sells mid-tier upgrades.
- **Calder's Reach** — Mining station near asteroids. Sells hull and reactor upgrades.
- **Hob's Landing** — Biggest Rim town. Sells combat upgrades.

**Concord Stations (safe but bureaucratic):**
- **Ostia Highport** — Largest port in the Core. Top-tier upgrades, expensive, requires clean record.
- **Concord Station Arbiter** — Customs station. Patrols ask questions.

**Dangerous Areas (avoid until upgraded):**
- **The Gauntlet** — Asteroid belt with Corsair pirates. Good salvage, frequent ambushes.
- **Blackwater Reach** — Corsair haven. Mined and fortified.
- **The Boneyard** — Settlement War battlefield. Relic drones guard valuable wrecks.
- **The Maw** — Hollow space. Deadly warships. Turn back.

**Mysterious Areas:**
- **The Shroud** — Vast nebula where sensors fail. Safe-ish, but things go missing.
- **Deep Shroud** — Uncharted. Strange signals. Risky.

## Station Roles Quick Reference

### Helm
**Main Job:** Get the ship where it needs to go without crashing.

**Controls:**
- **Impulse**: Slow, maneuverable local travel
- **Warp**: Fast travel between stations (drains energy)
- **Jump Drive** (if upgraded): Instant long-range jump (needs cooldown)
- **Docking**: Approach station slowly (under 100 speed), press "Request Dock", follow docking path

**What to Watch:**
- Asteroid fields (steer around or go slow)
- Nebulae (sensors reduced, fly carefully)
- Minefields (avoid!)
- Enemy positions (get beam weapons in range, keep missiles out of range)

**Tips:**
- Docking speed limit: ~100 units/sec
- Warp costs energy—Engineering watches the power
- Combat: turn to face enemies (beams fire forward), keep moving to spread damage across shields

---

### Weapons
**Main Job:** Destroy threats before they destroy you.

**Systems:**
- **Beam Weapons**: Auto-fire when enemies are in arc and range. You aim the ship (Helm turns, you manage power).
- **Missiles**: Load tube → Target enemy → Fire. Limited ammo (restock at stations).

**Missile Types:**
- **Homing**: Fire and forget, tracks target (most common)
- **Nuke**: Big damage, big radius (rare, expensive)
- **Mine**: Drops behind you (defensive)
- **EMP**: Disables enemy systems temporarily

**What to Watch:**
- Missile count (restock at friendly stations via Comms menu)
- Beam heat (overheat = offline, let them cool)
- Shield status (if shields down, you're taking hull damage)

**Tips:**
- Load tubes during quiet moments, not mid-fight
- Target weak enemies first (scouts, fighters)
- Save nukes for big threats (frigates, stations)
- Call out when missiles are inbound to the ship—Helm can dodge

---

### Engineering
**Main Job:** Keep the ship running when everything's on fire.

**Systems to Manage:**
- **Reactor**: Generates power
- **Shields**: Protect the hull (regenerate if given power)
- **Maneuver, Impulse, Warp, Jump**: Movement systems
- **Beam Weapons, Missile Tubes**: Combat systems
- **Scanners**: Science's sensors

**Controls:**
- **Power**: Slider 0-300%. More power = better performance but more heat.
- **Coolant**: Drag coolant from pool to hot systems. Cools them down.
- **Repair**: Send repair crews to damaged systems (takes time, crew can die if system hit again).

**What to Watch:**
- **Heat bars**: Orange = hot, red = overheating, flashing red = offline until cooled
- **Damage**: Systems turn yellow (damaged) or red (offline)
- **Power pool**: Don't overdraw—reactor can't keep up

**Tips:**
- Boost shields to 200-300% during combat
- Reduce power to non-critical systems (like warp) during fights to free up power
- Pre-cool weapons before a fight (max power + coolant)
- Damaged reactor = less total power available
- Call out system damage to Captain (e.g., "Warp drive is offline!")

---

### Science
**Main Job:** Know what's out there before it kills you.

**Controls:**
- **Scanning**: Click object → Hold scan button. Reveals details (faction, type, cargo, danger level).
- **Probe Launch**: Deploy sensor probes to scan distant objects or reveal areas (limited supply, restock at stations).

**What You Learn:**
- **Ships**: Faction, type, shields, hull strength
- **Stations**: Faction, services, if they're friendly
- **Salvage**: Value, contents (credits, missiles, equipment)
- **Anomalies**: Black holes, wormholes, derelicts with logs

**Scan Levels:**
- **Simple scan** (quick): Basic info, faction
- **Full scan** (takes time): Complete details, hidden cargo, derelict logs

**Tips:**
- Scan everything—it might be valuable salvage or a deadly warship
- Fully scan derelicts for story clues and extra salvage
- Call out threats: "Frigate at 12 o'clock, 15u, Corsair faction, shields up!"
- Some objects need multiple scans to reveal secrets

---

### Relay
**Main Job:** Big-picture awareness and communication.

**Screens:**
- **Sector map**: Shows all contacts, stations, waypoints
- **Database**: Ship types, factions, background info
- **Objectives**: Current mission goals
- **Comms log**: Messages from NPCs and stations

**What You Do:**
- **Set waypoints** for Helm (click map → "Set Waypoint")
- **Monitor threats**: Track enemy positions, warn Helm of ambushes
- **Comms**: Read incoming hails, relay intel to Captain
- **Reputation**: Check crew's standing with factions (displayed as "reputation points")

**Tips:**
- Zoom out to see the whole sector, zoom in for local threats
- Different colored contacts = different factions (learn the color code)
- Waypoints help Helm navigate complex routes
- Station hails often have dialogue choices—Captain decides, you click

---

### Operations (Optional 6th Station)
**Main Job:** Support Science and Relay.

**Capabilities:**
- Can scan like Science (useful in combat—Science focuses on threats, Ops scans salvage)
- Can manage comms like Relay
- Can see objectives and sector map

**Tips:**
- Use this station if you have 6 players
- Great for new players—less critical than other stations
- In combat: scan targets, call out enemy hull status
- In exploration: scan everything, find salvage

## How to Play

### 1. Docking at Stations
Stations are your lifeline: repairs, refuel, restock missiles, buy upgrades.

**How to Dock:**
1. **Relay**: Hail the station (Comms menu)
2. **Helm**: Approach slowly (under 100 speed)
3. **Helm**: Press "Request Dock" button
4. **Helm**: Follow the docking path overlay (stay in the funnel, it guides you in)
5. **Wait** for "Docked" message

**While Docked:**
- **Repairs**: Hull and systems auto-repair (costs energy, takes time)
- **Refuel**: Energy refills
- **Restock**: Missiles and probes replenish (may cost credits)
- **Upgrades**: Relay opens station menu → "Shipwright" → buy upgrades (if available)
- **Rumours**: Relay opens station menu → "Any news?" → station shares intel

**To Undock:**
- **Helm**: Press "Undock" button, wait for clearance, fly away

---

### 2. Space Combat

Combat is team coordination. Everyone has a role.

**Before Combat:**
- **Science**: Scan enemies (know what you're fighting)
- **Engineering**: Boost shields, pre-cool weapons
- **Weapons**: Load missile tubes
- **Helm**: Position for beam arcs

**During Combat:**
- **Helm**: Turn to keep beams on target, evade incoming missiles
- **Weapons**: Fire missiles at priority targets, manage beam heat
- **Engineering**: Balance power (shields vs weapons vs engines), route coolant, repair critical damage
- **Science**: Scan new threats, call out which enemy is weakest ("Fighter at 8 o'clock, shields down!")
- **Relay**: Track all enemies, warn of reinforcements

**After Combat:**
- **Helm**: Maneuver to salvage (Science scans it)
- **Engineering**: Repair damage, restore power balance
- **Relay**: Check if more enemies are incoming

**Fleeing:**
- **Helm**: Warp away (if not mass-locked by enemies) or jump (instant escape if jump drive charged)
- **Engineering**: Max power to engines, shields to rear
- **Weapons**: Mines (if equipped) cover your escape

---

### 3. Exploration and Salvage

**Salvage** is free money scattered across the universe.

**How to Salvage:**
1. **Science**: Scan debris/pods (reveals value: "Credits: 45")
2. **Helm**: Approach slowly (within 1u)
3. **Automatically collected** when close enough
4. Credits added to ship's funds (shown on Relay as "Reputation")

**Where to Find Salvage:**
- Asteroid fields
- Debris fields
- Derelict ships (scan fully for extra salvage)
- Random pods along trade lanes (freighters drop cargo sometimes)

**Value by Region:**
- Safe zones: 15-30 credits per pod
- Risky zones: 40-80 credits
- Dangerous zones: 100-150 credits
- Deadly zones: 150-200+ credits (if you survive)

**Survey Bonus:**
- First time entering a region? "Charting bonus" pays extra credits.

---

### 4. Traveling Long Distances

The Drift is big. You need efficient travel.

**Impulse** (slow, always available):
- ~0.1u per second
- Use for: docking, combat, local maneuvering
- Port Vesper to Halcyon Rest (32u): ~5-6 minutes

**Warp** (fast, drains energy):
- ~1-3u per second (depends on upgrades)
- Use for: station-to-station travel
- Same trip: ~20-60 seconds
- **Warning**: Can't warp if enemies are too close (mass-locked)

**Jump Drive** (instant, expensive upgrade):
- Instant teleport to any point within range (~30-50u)
- Use for: emergencies, long hauls, escaping combat
- **Cooldown**: ~90 seconds between jumps
- Costs 500 credits (Dustmote Salvage sells it)

**Nebulae:**
- Warp speed reduced inside nebulae
- Sensors reduced (harder to see threats)
- Good for hiding from enemies

## Making Money

You need credits to buy upgrades. Here's how to earn:

### 1. Salvage (Easiest)
- Scan debris fields, collect pods
- Safe zones: slow and steady income
- Dangerous zones: big payouts, high risk

### 2. Missions (Campaign-Specific)
- Story campaigns: missions pay 200-600 credits
- Sandbox campaigns: GM may offer delivery/escort jobs

### 3. Trade Rumors (If GM/AI Director Offers)
- Stations may hint: "Station X needs cargo Y, will pay well"
- Buy cargo at one station, sell at another
- Profit margins vary

### 4. Combat Bounties (Rare)
- Some campaigns pay for destroying pirates
- Usually incidental—don't hunt combat for money

### 5. Survey Bonuses
- First visit to a region: small credit bonus for "charting"
- Encourages exploration

**Credits Display:**
- Shown on **Relay** screen as "Reputation Points"
- Persists across sessions (campaign save)

## Upgrades

**16 upgrades** available across the universe. Each station sells different ones.

### Where to Buy
Ask stations via Comms menu: "Shipwright" option shows their inventory.

**Port Vesper** (starting location):
- Hull Plating, Missile Racks, Repair Crew, Engine Tune, Forward Beams

**Halcyon Rest** (Shroud edge):
- Shield MK2, Sensor Suite, Repair Crew, Reactor Overhaul

**Hob's Landing** (Rim):
- Beam Coils, Combat Thrusters, Warp Overhaul, Shield MK2

**Dustmote Salvage** (black market):
- **Jump Drive**, Heavy Beams, Front Tube, Hull Plating 2

**Ostia Highport** (Core, expensive):
- Shield MK3, Sensor Suite, Engine Tune, Warp Overhaul, Combat Thrusters, Reactor Overhaul
- **Refuses service if you're wanted by Concord**

### Upgrade Priorities

**Early Game (first 300-500 credits):**
1. **Hull Plating** (150cr) — More hit points
2. **Shield MK2** (200cr) — Better shields
3. **Repair Crew** (150cr) — Faster repairs

**Mid Game (500-1000 credits):**
4. **Jump Drive** (500cr) — Escape tool, travel efficiency
5. **Forward Beams** or **Heavy Beams** (300cr) — More damage
6. **Combat Thrusters** (300cr) — Better maneuverability

**Late Game (exploring dangerous zones):**
7. **Hull Plating 2** (400cr) — Tough hull
8. **Shield MK3** (400cr) — Military-grade shields
9. **Warp Overhaul** (400cr) — Faster travel
10. **Reactor Overhaul** (500cr) — More power for everything

## Danger Zones

Each region has a **danger level** displayed on Helm, Relay, and Operations screens.

### Safe (Green)
- **The Core**: Concord patrols keep it safe
- **The Border**: Port Vesper area, busy trade lanes
- **The Rim**: Hob's Landing, most frontier zones
- **The Shroud** (outer edge): Nebula reduces sensors but few hostiles

**Safe for:** Stock ships, new crews

---

### Risky (Yellow)
- **The Gauntlet**: Asteroid belt with Corsair ambush packs (small groups: 2-3 fighters/scouts)
- **Deep Shroud**: Uncharted nebula, strange signals, occasional hostile

**Requires:** 2-3 upgrades (hull, shields, weapons)
**Rewards:** Better salvage (40-80 credits per pod)

---

### Dangerous (Orange)
- **Blackwater Reach**: Corsair haven, minefields, heavy patrols (frigates + fighters)
- **The Boneyard**: Settlement War battlefield, relic war drones guard wrecks (tough, aggressive)

**Requires:** 5+ upgrades (jump drive, heavy beams, shield mk3)
**Rewards:** Excellent salvage (100-150 credits), rare equipment
**Warning:** Don't go here in a stock ship—you'll die

---

### Deadly (Red)
- **The Maw**: Hollow territory with heavy warships and defense platforms
- **Turn-back warnings** appear on all screens when you approach

**Requires:** 8+ upgrades (basically everything)
**Rewards:** Legendary salvage (150-200+ credits), but is it worth your life?
**Warning:** Even fully upgraded, this is a challenge

## Factions

### Independent (Gray)
- **You**, most traders, Port Vesper, Halcyon Rest, Hob's Landing
- Neutral to each other
- Can dock at Independent stations freely

### Concord (Blue)
- The law, the government, the winners of the Settlement War
- Stations: Ostia Highport, Lumen Exchange, Arbiter Line checkpoints
- **Neutral** unless you attack them or flee from inspections
- **Wanted status**: If tagged, Concord stations refuse service and patrols attack
- **How to get wanted**: Attack Concord ships, run from customs inspections, ignore lawful orders

### Corsairs (Red)
- Pirates, raiders, opportunists
- Station: Blackwater (hostile territory)
- **Always hostile** to traders
- Ambush in the Gauntlet and Blackwater Reach
- Avoid fair fights—they flee if outmatched

### Relic (Yellow)
- War drones from the Settlement War, still fighting 20 years later
- Found in: The Boneyard
- **Hostile to everyone**
- Don't negotiate, don't respond to hails, just destroy threats

### Hollow (Purple)
- Unknown faction from the Maw
- **Extremely hostile, mysterious**
- Heavy warships, defense platforms
- Spacers tell stories, nobody confirms them

### Derelict (Gray, no icon)
- Dead ships, abandoned stations
- Neutral, can be salvaged
- **Scan fully** to read logs (story clues)

## Tips for New Crews

### Before Your First Session
1. **Read this guide** (or at least your station's section)
2. **Watch a 5-minute EmptyEpsilon tutorial** on YouTube (search "EmptyEpsilon station guide")
3. **Agree on a Captain** (they don't have a console, they coordinate)

### During Your First Session
1. **Start docked at Port Vesper** (safe)
2. **Practice docking/undocking** before leaving the safe zone
3. **Hail Port Vesper** for rumours (Relay comms menu)
4. **Buy one upgrade** if you have credits (hull plating or shields)
5. **Visit nearby stations**: Calder's Reach (14u south), Halcyon Rest (32u east)
6. **Scan everything** (Science) to learn what's what
7. **Avoid combat** until you're comfortable flying
8. **If you see Corsairs**, run (warp away to nearest station)

### Communication Tips
- **Call out everything**: "Asteroid ahead," "Enemy at 3 o'clock," "Shields down," "Warp drive damaged"
- **Use clock positions**: 12 = straight ahead, 6 = behind, 3 = right, 9 = left
- **Captain decides**, stations execute
- **Don't panic**—you'll die sometimes, that's part of the game

### Common Mistakes
- ❌ **Docking too fast** → Crash damage, failed dock
- ❌ **Not scanning** → Fly into danger, miss salvage
- ❌ **Overdrawing power** (Engineering) → Reactor can't keep up, systems shut down
- ❌ **Fighting without shields** → Hull damage is expensive to repair
- ❌ **Warping into asteroid fields** → Collision damage
- ❌ **Visiting the Maw early** → Death

## Common Questions

### Q: How do I know where to go?
**A:** Relay can hail stations for rumours, or your campaign/GM gives you objectives. In sandbox mode, you decide—explore, salvage, or follow hints.

### Q: What happens if we die?
**A:** Session ends (usually). Campaign saves your credits/upgrades up to that point, so you don't lose everything. Some campaigns have retry options.

### Q: Do credits carry between sessions?
**A:** Yes! Campaigns save credits, upgrades, and story progress in the server's save file. You pick up where you left off.

### Q: Can we switch stations mid-session?
**A:** Technically yes (disconnect and reconnect to different station), but it's disruptive. Better to commit for the session.

### Q: How long is a session?
**A:** Depends on campaign:
- **Story campaigns**: 45-75 minutes per session
- **Sandbox**: Flexible, 30 minutes to 3 hours

### Q: What's the AI director?
**A:** An optional local language model (runs on a separate PC) that creates dynamic encounters, voices NPCs, and responds to GM instructions. Makes the universe feel alive. Not required—scenarios work fine without it.

### Q: Can we play without the AI director?
**A:** Yes! Every campaign works in Director=Off mode. You get scripted encounters instead of dynamic ones. Still fun, just less improvisation.

### Q: What if we want to fight each other (PvP)?
**A:** Some scenarios support multiple player ships. Ask your GM—sandbox mode can easily add a second ship.

### Q: Is voice chat required?
**A:** Highly recommended. Discord, Zoom, or in-person. Text chat works but is much slower.

### Q: Can I play solo?
**A:** EmptyEpsilon supports it (you switch between stations), but it's designed for 3-6 players. Solo is hard.
